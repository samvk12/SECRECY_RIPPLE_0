01 · CORE ARGUMENT

secrecy is the default shield. it also blocks the view.

Model weights, training data and scoring logic are rarely patentable or worth publishing in a patent, so firms keep them as trade secrets. That same secrecy blocks the scrutiny that fairness, safety and public trust need. The law is moving to controlled disclosure: secrets stay protected from competitors, while regulators, courts and affected people see enough to hold the system to account.

        05 · BUSINESS AND MANAGERIAL IMPLICATIONS

        secret by default is no longer safe by default.

        Plan disclosure

        Two audiences, two files

        Plan for disclosure to regulators (documentation, training-data summaries, explanations) and keep it separate from what competitors can see.

        Prove care

        Show reasonable steps

        Access logs, NDAs, clean-room onboarding. X.AI v. OpenAI and Apple v. Liu turn on whether the plaintiff can identify specific secrets and prove misuse.

        Choose protection

        Patent or secret

        Patents publish the invention. Secrecy avoids that but invites transparency mandates and leaves no protection after a leak.

        Compete on trust

        Explainability sells

        EU fines (GPAI up to 3% of turnover or EUR 15m) and lost public trust make explainability a competitive feature. Indian firms selling into the EU must comply regardless.
